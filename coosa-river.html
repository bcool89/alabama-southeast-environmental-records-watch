Priority 3 + Priority 6 · AL-SE-010 / AL-SE-032

Coosa River Watershed

An interstate source-water and environmental-records investigation spanning the Georgia headwaters, Coosa River reservoirs, the former Alabama Army Ammunition Plant and downstream drinking-water facilities.

Evidence standard: Historical detections, incomplete assessments or potential transport routes do not establish current exposure, regulatory noncompliance or an emergency. Conditions must be checked against dated original records.

Why investigate this corridor?

The Coosa River system connects upstream Georgia tributaries with Alabama reservoirs and water-supply infrastructure. Public records describe historical industrial and military environmental issues in parts of this watershed. That geographic relationship is a basis for research—not proof that one site caused detections in any particular water system.

Georgia headwaters and Weiss Lake

Compile Georgia EPD and EPA PFAS water-quality records, sampling dates, tributary sites and reported results. Verify transport hypotheses with hydrological evidence.

Georgia EPD PFAS information

Alabama reservoirs and intakes

Compile ambient surface-water sampling, utility source-water assessments and treated-water monitoring. Distinguish source-water contamination from tap-water exposure.

AL-SE-010 · Former Alabama Army Ammunition Plant

The former Childersburg-area defense facility is documented in EPA's Superfund cleanup records. Investigate its historical soil, sediment and groundwater contamination, previous remediation decisions and 2023 five-year review, while verifying the status of any remaining work.

Source-attribution safeguards

Do not infer that Georgia industrial facilities, the former Army ammunition plant or any other specific source caused Shelby County PFAS detections without compound profiles, sampling chronology, watershed modeling and supporting records. Where evidence is missing, the finding remains an open question.
