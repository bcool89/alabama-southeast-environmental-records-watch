Priority 1 · AL-SE-001 / AL-SE-002 / AL-SE-003

Chelsea & Shelby County

An evidence-led review of local drinking-water PFAS results, treatment and source-water questions, industrial cleanup history, and records needed to establish current conditions.

Evidence standard: Historical detections, incomplete assessments or potential transport routes do not establish current exposure, regulatory noncompliance or an emergency. Conditions must be checked against dated original records.

Documented starting point: Shelby County Water Services

The utility's 2024 annual drinking-water quality report contains PFAS monitoring data for two treatment plants. This establishes historical detections in reported samples, not residents' current exposures or a present regulatory violation. Comparisons against legal standards require the applicable sampling window, averaging provisions and compliance dates.

Shelby County South treatment plant

Review 2024 report data and obtain current raw-water and treated-water results for the Coosa River / Lay Lake source area.

Talladega/Shelby treatment plant

Obtain updated PFAS concentrations and source-water documentation for the Childersburg-area facility.

Primary source: Shelby County 2024 drinking-water quality report · Shelby County Water Services

Open research questions

  1. What do the 2025 and 2026 sample results show at each treatment plant?
  2. Which results represent raw water, finished water or distribution-system water?
  3. Which areas are served by which water sources, and do sources change seasonally?
  4. What treatment improvements have been planned, funded, installed or tested?
  5. What upstream contamination sources have actually been verified by sampling and hydrological studies?

Exposure pathway cautions

The location of an upstream cleanup site does not establish that its contaminants are present in a particular household's water. Treatment, transport, dilution, sampling method, time and household water supply all matter. Private wells require separate assessment from public utility monitoring.
