# Evidence and records standards

- Maintain stable site and request identifiers; do not reuse IDs.
- Record the original source, publication date, sampling date, medium, units, method, reporting limits and any applicable regulatory standard before publishing numerical findings.
- Separate historical contamination, documented detections, assessment-only records, screening leads, verified current exposure and official emergency notices. A site listing or a single detection is not proof of a current emergency or a legal violation.
- Establish geographic relevance via documented pathways, not proximity alone. Do not attribute contaminants to a specific source without supporting evidence.
- Search existing public records first. Prefer final reports, analytical datasets and named documents; limit date ranges and ask for existing electronic files. Require an itemized estimate and written authorization before fees. For federal FOIA, consider a supported public-interest fee-waiver request.
- Track request status, custodian, submission date, due date, response date, cost and public response links. Never mark a proposed request submitted without evidence.
- Publish only releasable material; protect private information and omit sensitive security-related information. Retain public source links and log corrections. Do not imply affiliation with government agencies.
