Our mission

Improve public access to environmental records, prioritize local relevance, identify unanswered questions and use targeted requests to reduce costs. This regional initiative is separate from the national Hazardous Stockpile Records Watch project. It is not a government agency or an emergency alert service.

Evidence before conclusions. A listed site is not necessarily unsafe. Historical findings are not current exposure determinations; a plausible pathway is not proof of exposure. This starter inventory is not a comprehensive current emergency assessment.

Research and publication standards

Evidence categories

Documented historical detections; historical contamination; assessment published; screening lead. Future entries may distinguish verified current contamination, remediation or controlled exposure, and official emergency notices. These categories are not severity ratings.

Exposure and compliance

Record sample dates, units, medium, analytical method and applicable standards. Separate detection, exceedance of a comparison value, regulatory noncompliance and actual exposure. Never infer a current emergency from historical records.

Cost control

Search existing public archives; ask informally for named documents; request indexes if needed; then submit narrowly scoped requests. Prefer electronic copies and require a fee estimate before authorizing charges.

Corrections and privacy

Retain source provenance and update history. Publish only releasable environmental records; redact private information and security-sensitive details. Report source corrections through the repository's issue tracker; do not include confidential information.
